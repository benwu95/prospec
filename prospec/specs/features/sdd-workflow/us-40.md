## US-40: Reuse Is Adjudicated Before a New Implementation Is Planned [P1]

As an AI executor or reviewer running the plan and review stations on any project,
I want the plan station to adjudicate each new surface against the project's existing owners — and standard plans to argue a simpler alternative — before code is written,
so that re-implementing a knowledge-base-documented single-source helper is caught on a one-page plan rather than paid for as a review critical on a thousand-line diff.

**Acceptance Scenarios:**
- WHEN the Architecture Verifier audits a plan, THEN a fifth dimension, Reuse & Single-Source, requires every new writer / creator / parser / formatter surface to name its existing owner with evidence or to argue the rewrite explicitly, and grades an unargued bypass FLAWS
- WHEN a `standard`-scale plan is written, THEN it carries a Simpler Alternative section — a materially simpler alternative or an explicit concession — with a files/lines change-surface estimate, and the plan skill's Phase 4 gate checks for it
- WHEN a review finds a knowledge-base-documented single-source helper re-implemented on an autonomous or write path, THEN the severity contract grades it critical, defined once in `review-format.md` and cited by name from the rubric and the lens reference

**Independent Test:**
Render `plan-verifier-rubric`, `plan-format`, `prospec-plan`, `cascade-protocol`, `review-format`, `review-lenses-content` and `prospec-review`; assert the five-dimension rubric, the Section 8 skeleton, the shared title strings, the critical #4 definition living only in `review-format`, and that no rendered surface names a prospec-internal service or helper.

### Behavior Specifications

#### REQ-TEMPLATES-200: Simpler Alternative Section in plan-format
The `plan-format.md` reference defines a `### 8. Simpler Alternative` section that a `standard`-scale `plan.md` must carry: either a materially simpler alternative or an explicit one-sentence concession with its reason, plus a change-surface estimate table in files and lines order of magnitude — the chosen approach always, the alternative side by side when one is proposed (a concession names the nearest alternative considered or omits that row with its reason) — so that "simpler" is a comparable downstream review/verify cost claim.
- WHEN `plan-format.md` is rendered, THEN it defines the `### 8. Simpler Alternative` section with the alternative-or-concede rule, its `## Simpler Alternative` skeleton, and a change-surface estimate table (`Approach` / `Files` / `Lines` columns; a `Chosen:` row always, an `Alternative:` row when one is proposed or as the nearest alternative considered when conceding)
- WHEN the Scale Tiers table describes `standard`, THEN it names `## Simpler Alternative` as a required section; WHEN it describes `full`, THEN it states that the tournament's recorded non-selected candidates stand in for the section
- WHEN measured for knowledge token budget, THEN the rendered `plan-format.md` remains $\le 2500$ tokens

#### REQ-TEMPLATES-201: prospec-plan Carries the Reuse Gate at Phase 4 and Phase 6

- WHEN Phase 4 lists the plan.md sections and its gate, THEN both name `Simpler Alternative` as required under `standard` (or absent scale), deferring the format to `references/plan-format.md` Section 8; the Scale-tiered depth summary names it for `standard` and the tournament stand-in for `full`
- WHEN Phase 4 describes Implementation Steps, THEN it instructs the author to name the existing owner delegated to (or the negative search result) or argue the rewrite for every new writer / creator / parser / formatter surface — the authoring counterpart of the rubric's Reuse & Single-Source dimension, deferring to `references/plan-format.md` Section 6
- WHEN Phase 6 Step 1 enumerates the rubric dimensions, THEN it lists five, the fifth named `Reuse & Single-Source`, and the Phase 6 gate counts five dimensions
- WHEN the shared titles `Reuse & Single-Source` and `Simpler Alternative` appear in the skill, THEN they are byte-identical to the rubric's and plan-format's headings — the fifth dimension title is compared against the rendered rubric heading by a contract test, and both titles are pinned by one shared contract-test constant
